      <article aria-labelledby="pageload-heading">
        <h3 id="pageload-heading">How apple.com/store loads</h3>
        <figure>
          <img src="images/page-load.png" alt="Simplified timeline of the apple.com/store page load, marking DOMContentLoaded at 712 milliseconds and full load at 1.63 seconds" width="480" height="270">
          <figcaption>DOMContentLoaded at 712&nbsp;ms, full load at 1.63&nbsp;s.</figcaption>
        </figure>
        <p>
          Using the Network panel in Chrome DevTools, I traced every step of loading
          <a href="https://www.apple.com/store" rel="noopener">apple.com/store</a>: the
          <abbr title="Domain Name System">DNS</abbr> lookup, the request and response with Apple's servers, and
          every asset the page pulled in afterward.
        </p>
        <table>
          <caption>Resources loaded, by type</caption>
          <thead>
            <tr>
              <th scope="col">Type</th>
              <th scope="col">Requests</th>
              <th scope="col">Size</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody>
            <tr><th scope="row">Images</th><td>65</td><td>289&nbsp;kB</td></tr>
            <tr><th scope="row">Fonts</th><td>7</td><td>825&nbsp;kB</td></tr>
            <tr><th scope="row">Fetch/XHR</th><td>7</td><td>2,373&nbsp;kB</td></tr>
          </tbody>
          <tfoot>
            <tr><th scope="row">All requests</th><td>120</td><td>4.6&nbsp;MB</td></tr>
          </tfoot>
        </table>
        <details>
          <summary>What I learned</summary>
          <p>
            Images made up more than half of the requests but barely any of the weight. The 7 fonts were almost
            three times heavier than all 65 images combined, and the 7 data requests were the heaviest category of all.
          </p>
        </details>
      </article>
